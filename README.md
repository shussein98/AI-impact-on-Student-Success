# AI Impact on Student Academic Success
It seems that overnight Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) has made its way into schools and learning institutions. As administrators scramble to implement and enforce policies it is important to critically analyze the impact on students and the possible benefit of AI on students. The dataset analyzed 50,000 students, collecting data on grade point average (GPA), self-reported ratings of anxiety and knowledge as well as behavioral patterns. 
Note: All data in this project is entirely synthetic and created for demonstration purposes.

**Objective:**
The purpose of this analysis project was to investigate and analyze how AI usage affects student success and find any correlation between success and existing policies. The key metrics that were used were the difference in pre and post semester GPAs, self-reported student anxiety and skill retention. 

**Executive Summary:**
While usage might not necessarily translate to increased success, students with more advanced prompt engineering skills had the highest increase in gpa over the course of the measured semester. Conversely, among students surveyed those that had the highest usage of AI tools had the most anxiety when it came to exams and testing. We can most likely assume that this anxiety could be in part due to the fact that exams test students' knowledge, recall and ability to apply learned concepts. It may be easy to draw the conclusion that students that use AI tools more had higher skill retention scores-meaning that AI use had a positive impact on scores. However, it is important to consider that a possible explanation for this correlation could be that students that possess strong prompt engineering skills also possess strong retention skills. 
Moving forward I would recommend that institutions look to implement tools that detect AI, specifically institutions that seek to implement strict AI policies. Furthermore, assignments that challenge students' application of concepts would better evaluate students' knowledge of the course material.
